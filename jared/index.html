Trick 1 · Live on this slide
Things are gonna get weird.
No video. No plugin. No JavaScript. About forty lines of CSS. In the room it fires every six to nine seconds, so people are never sure they saw it.
Trick 1 · How it works
Three layers on one word.
LAYER 1Scanlines

The word is filled with thin horizontal stripes that drift slowly down, like an old monitor.

LAYER 2Two ghosts

Two hidden copies of the word sit on top: one cyan, one magenta. Almost always invisible.

LAYER 3Rare glitches

For a tenth of a second, thin slices of the ghosts jump sideways. Three timers that never line up.

The rule that makes it work on stage: one word per slide, a few slides per talk. Use it on every slide and it becomes noise.
Trick 1 · The prompt
Paste this into Claude, with your slide.
Add a "CRT flicker" accent to the highlighted word in my slide headline. Pure CSS, no libraries, no JavaScript.

1. Wrap the word like this: <span class="crt-accent" data-text="WORD">WORD</span>
2. Scanlines: fill the text with a repeating horizontal gradient (2px of the accent colour, 1px of a darker shade), clipped to the letters, drifting slowly downwards.
3. Ghosts: add two copies of the word with ::before and ::after, using content: attr(data-text). One cyan, one magenta. They are invisible almost all the time.
4. Glitches: every 6 to 9 seconds, for about a tenth of a second, show thin horizontal slices of each ghost shifted sideways by a few hundredths of an em. Use clip-path: inset() and steps() timing. At the same moment, nudge the main word with a tiny skew and a brightness change.
5. Give the three animations different durations so the glitches never repeat in sync.
6. It must stay readable from the back of a room: the word never disappears and never moves more than 0.05em.
7. If the viewer has "reduce motion" turned on, show the plain coloured word.

Show me the result as one HTML file, on a dark background, with this headline: "Things are gonna get weird."

Trick 2 · The prompt
A guided tour over one big picture.
Build one slide that works as a guided camera tour over a single large picture, like Prezi. One HTML file, no libraries.

- The picture is one wide canvas (I will give you the content: a diagram, a map, a timeline or a photo).
- The slide shows the canvas inside a window. Each press of the right arrow moves a "camera": it pans and zooms smoothly (about 1.5 seconds, ease-out) to the next stop. The left arrow goes back one stop.
- Define the stops as a simple list I can edit: centre x, centre y, zoom level, and a one-line caption shown under the window.
- First stop: the whole canvas. Last stop: the whole canvas again.
- Small details should be too small to read in the overview and comfortable to read at their stop.
- Never zoom so far that text looks blurred; use real text or vector shapes, not a low-resolution image.
- If the viewer has "reduce motion" turned on, jump between stops without animation.

Content for the canvas: [describe it here, or attach the image].
Stops: [list the 3 to 6 things you want to visit, in order].

Trick 3 · The prompt
A chart people can touch.
Build one slide with an interactive chart that I can operate live with the mouse while I talk. One HTML file, no libraries, drawn in SVG.

- The chart shows one curve: [what goes on the vertical axis] as a function of [what goes on the horizontal axis].
- The formula is: [write the rule in plain words, with the numbers].
- There is one large dot on the curve that I can drag left and right with the mouse or a finger. The dot follows the curve.
- Next to the chart, show three numbers that update as I drag, and one big verdict word that changes colour when the result flips (for example WIN in green, LOSE in red).

Mac Mini
The setup

Five stops. About thirty minutes.

This is your office. Press → to walk through it.

Stop 1 · The Mac Mini

Make sure it never sleeps.

  1. Open System Settings and go to Energy.
  2. Stop it sleeping when the display is off. The wording varies by macOS version.
  3. Turn on “Start up automatically after a power failure”.
Stop 2 · One folder

Give Claude a place to work.

  1. Create one folder, for example Documents/Claude.
  2. Put there the files you want it to use.
  3. Claude works inside this folder, not in your whole home folder.
Stop 3 · The Claude app

Install it and sign in.

  1. Download the desktop app from claude.ai/download.
  2. Sign in with your Claude account.
  3. Settings, then Desktop app: turn on “Keep computer awake”.
  4. Add Claude to Login Items, so it opens after a restart.
Stop 4 · Remote Control

Switch it on.

  1. Open the Code tab and start a session in your folder.
  2. In the session details, Environment card, turn on “Remote Control”. Or type /remote-control.
  3. Leave this session open. Your phone will talk to it.
Stop 5 · Your phone

Find the Mac from your pocket.

  1. Install the Claude app. Same account.
  2. Tap Code.
  3. Pick the session with a computer icon and a green dot. Type a task and send it.
The test

Walk out. Switch off wifi. Send a task.

If it answers, it works. From the Strip, or from anywhere else.

Staying in control
How much do you let it do on its own?
START HEREManual

Claude asks before each action. The question arrives on your phone and you tap to approve.

WHEN YOU TRUST ITAccept edits

It changes files in the folder without asking. It still asks before running anything else.

FOR LONG TASKSPlan, then Auto

Plan: it proposes the steps and waits for your yes. Auto: it runs with fewer questions.

A question you do not answer expires after about five minutes, and the task waits. If you want an extra lock, turn on "Require trusted devices" in the settings: the phone then asks for Face ID or fingerprint from time to time.
When it does not answer
Three causes cover almost every case.
  • ASLEEP
    The Mac went to sleep.Check stop 1 and the "Keep computer awake" switch.
  • CLOSED
    The Claude app, or that session, is closed.Sessions only run while the app is open. Reopen it and switch Remote Control on again.
  • OFFLINE
    The home internet dropped.It reconnects on its own when the connection returns.
When you travel, leave a way to restart it: someone at home, or a smart plug.
Your first week
Three tasks to send from your phone.
  • MON
    "Read the three PDFs in the folder and give me the five points I should challenge in class."
  • WED
    "Turn my notes in notes.txt into a ten-slide deck in one HTML file. Use the flicker on one word."
  • FRI
    "Compare this year's syllabus with last year's and list what changed."
Put the files in the Claude folder first. Start with tasks where a mistake costs nothing, and read what it did before you rely on it.
One step further
Tasks that start by themselves.
ON THE MAC MINIScheduled tasks

In the Code tab, open Scheduled and create a new task. Choose "Only on this computer". It runs while the app is open and the Mac is awake.

WITHOUT THE MACCloud tasks

Created at claude.ai/code/routines. They run on Anthropic's computers, even with the Mac off. They work on code stored in GitHub, so they are less useful for your own files.

Example for the Mac: every Monday at 7:00, "read the new files in the folder and write me a one-page summary".
